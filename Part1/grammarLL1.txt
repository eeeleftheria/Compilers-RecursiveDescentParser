TRANSFORMATIONS
-----------------------
1st transformation: precedences
    - based on the lectures' slides (specifically the transformation
    from slide 9 to slide 19 of the syntax analysis unit), I
    created a non-terminal <term> which produces ** deeper in the grammar,
    thus with higher priority. Basically, term is evaluated first as part of
    the evaluation of <exp>, so ** is of greater priority than /.  


1. exp -> term 
2.     | exp / term 

3. term -> term ** factor
4.     | factor

5. factor -> str
6.     | (exp)

7. str -> char 
8.     | char str

9. char -> a-z 
10.     | A-Z



2nd transformation: associativity
    - ** is already left associative since in can be produces
    through left recursion, while the recursion of rule #2 should be swapped
    since we need term to appear on the right side as a right associative so 
    the right operations are evaluated first.

1. exp -> term 
2.     | term / exp 

3. term -> term ** factor
4.     | factor

5. factor -> str
6.     | (exp)

7. str -> char 
8.     | char str

9. char -> a-z 
10.     | A-Z



3rd transformation: left factoring
    - FIST(#1) = FIRST(#2) and FIRST(#7) = FIRST(#8),
    so the first part which is similar in more than one rule
    should be shared in a single rule and then the suffix should
    be determined by the other rules.

1. exp -> term expTail 

2. expTail -> / exp
3.        | ε

4. term -> term ** factor
5.     | factor

6. factor -> str
7.     | (exp)

8. str -> char strTail 

9. strTail -> str
10.        | ε

11. char -> a-z 
12.     | A-Z




4th transformation: elimination of left recursion
    - left recursion appears only in rule #4, since
    it is of type A->*Aa

1. exp -> term expTail 

2. expTail -> / exp
3.        | ε

4. term -> factor termTail

5. termTail -> ** factor termTail
6.      | ε

7. factor -> str
8.     | (exp)

9. str -> char strTail 

10. strTail -> str
11.        | ε

12. char -> a-z 
13.     | A-Z
